Media type

Digital audio · 1992

MiniDisc

Sony’s pocket-sized rewritable music format. You could jump to a song as on a CD, then rearrange the tracks on the disc. Early recordable MiniDiscs held up to 74 minutes.

Look for
A square shell with a sliding shutter. The disc stays inside.
Read with
An MD deck or portable player. For later Hi-MD recordings, check for Hi-MD support.
Sony’s development history

Computer storage · 1994–95

Zip disk

Iomega’s original 100 MB cartridge gave people room to swap graphics and back up folders without spreading them over a stack of floppies.

Look for
The Zip name and a capacity printed on the label. This example is a 100 MB disk.
Read with
An Iomega Zip drive that supports the disk’s capacity. Check its connector, too.
Computer History Museum collection record

Analog audio · 1969

Microcassette

Olympus introduced the format with its Zuiko Pearlcorder. The tapes are small enough to run in a shirt-pocket recorder, handy for dictation or a quick interview.

Look for
Two exposed reel hubs and a tiny side letter. The example here is an XB60.
Read with
A Microcassette recorder. Try its other speed if speech plays too fast or too slow.
Olympus’s product chronology

Analog video · 1985

Video8

Sony’s CCD-V8 brought standard 8 mm tape to home camcorders in 1985. It was a smaller cassette to pack for holidays and school plays than the full-size tapes used in many home decks.

Look for
A rectangular flap over the tape and an “8” or “Video8” mark on the case.
Read with
A Video8 camcorder or deck. For newer 8 mm hardware, check the manual for analog Video8 playback.
